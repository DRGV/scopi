ImagePi - Pi 5 test 6
=====================

What test5 and test5c showed
PASSED on the Pi:
- The Preview box works (Gaussian slider and typed Sigma, Median, Auto Threshold, an 18 MP image without freezing); Cancel restores the picture.
- "Process entire stack": ticked filters every frame, unticked filters only the frame that is showing; Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Shift+Z, a two-step Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Z from the
  Results table and Edit > Undo all restore the data exactly. (The first run of part C looked odd, most likely a mix-up of the two boxes, and nothing on screen
  said what a filter or an Undo had done. That is the first change below.)
- Shift-drag lines read 0 left to right and 45 up-right.

CHANGED on the Mac, none of it on the Pi yet:
1. The status line (bottom of the window, shown for 5 seconds) now says what happened:
     after a filter on a stack:  "Gaussian Blur: 0.06 s, frame 3 of 3 only"  or  "Gaussian Blur: 0.06 s, all 3 frames"
     after Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Shift+Z: "Undo: Gaussian Blur", "Redo: Gaussian Blur", or "Nothing to undo" / "Nothing to redo"
   On a single image the filter message is as before. Some operations (Auto Threshold) change the pixel type and always convert every frame, whatever the box
   says; the message tells you what really happened.
2. The frame slider under a stack: a click in the groove or one turn of the mouse wheel moves ONE frame (it jumped to the last one), and it shows tick marks.
3. A big picture at Fit zoom only gets its middle part (about 1200 x 1200 pixels) previewed. That part now has a dashed frame labelled "Preview area".
4. A Shift-drag line pointing left now always reads exactly 180 (it read 180 or -180 depending on which side of the line the pointer ended), and straight up or
   down reads exactly 90 or -90.

Rules

PART E - Shift-dragged lines (about 5 minutes)
----------------------------------------------

E1. File > Open ~/imagepi-test/known_shapes.png. Analyze > Show Results, click Clear. Choose the Line tool in the toolbar.
    Hold Shift and drag from left to right across a square, then press Ctrl+M.                                       -> Angle 0
E2. Hold Shift and drag from right to left across a square and end the pointer a few pixels ABOVE the line; Ctrl+M.     -> Angle 180
    Do it again and end the pointer a few pixels BELOW the line; Ctrl+M.                                              -> Angle 180 (it used to be -180)
    Do both once more, so you have four right-to-left rows.
E3. Hold Shift and drag straight upward, Ctrl+M (-> 90); straight downward, Ctrl+M (-> -90); upward to the right at about 45 degrees, Ctrl+M (-> 45).
    (Without Shift a hand-drawn line pointing left still reads about 180 or -180 by which side the pointer ended: that is normal.)
In the Results panel click "Save CSV..." and send me the file's text (8 rows: Angle is the column to read).
