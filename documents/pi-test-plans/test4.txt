PROBLEMS found, and what I changed (all on the Mac, none tested on the Pi yet):
1. Orientation. Without ticks the picture was the paper mirrored left-to-right; the eyepiece shows the paper rotated 180 degrees; Flip vertically
   alone matched the eyepiece. You want no ticks to mean "same as the eyepiece" (USB cable pointing to the rear of the microscope). Done: the camera
   backend now knows its frames arrive upside down (delivered_flip), and the two boxes are changes relative to the eyepiece view. Your saved
   "Flip horizontally" tick is not reused (the settings keys were renamed), so both boxes start unticked.
2. Crop with Flip horizontally ticked came from the wrong place. From a.png / a-cropped.png: x was right, but the crop started at about y 42 where you
   selected y 196 (of 922): 922 - 196 - 684 = 42, i.e. the vertically mirrored place. So the camera's hardware crop is measured in the eyepiece view's
   coordinates while its frames arrive upside down; the app now maps the selection that way. This is an inference from one screenshot: roitest (Part B)
   measures it on the camera directly. The second crop being off was the same error (the first crop was not where Scopi thought).
3. A second snap in the same live view timed out; stopping and starting the live view fixed it. The camera backend now restarts the stream after
   every still (about a second, the live view carries on by itself), and once more if a still does not arrive; the error now lists the SDK events seen.
   snaptest (Part C) repeats snaps and shows what the SDK itself does.
4. Gaussian Blur: the slider showed no change in preview mode. On the Mac the slider did request previews, so the likely cause is that the dialog waited
   for the drag to pause before asking, and the picture only showed the newest request. Now the preview follows the drag about four times a second.
   If it still shows nothing on the Pi I need to know whether typing a Sigma value works (Part D4).
5. Line measurements: the Results row had empty X, Y and Unit columns and no pixel length. Now it has X, Y (the line's midpoint), Unit and "Length (px)",
   and Set Scale reports "Scale: 1 pixel = ... mm (... pixels per mm)" in the status line, so you can see the pixel size it computed.
6. The overlay printed "exposure 21.052 ms"; it now shows one decimal. pi_bringup.py render no longer prints the Wayland textinput line.
Noted for later (in the readme, not changed now): the main window cannot be made shorter than its panels need, the outline colour and width should
become user-selectable.

PART B - Where does a hardware crop land? (command line, about 1 minute, camera only)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

B1. Nothing may hold the camera: pgrep -af "scopi|pi_bringup|toupcam" (no output except the pgrep line). Lamp on, lens cap off, the tissue-paper
    slide under the lens (a still, textured picture; nothing may move while it runs), focus it roughly.
B2. python scripts/pi_bringup.py roitest 2>&1 | tee ~/roitest.log
    It grabs a full frame, asks the camera for an off-centre crop, finds the cropped picture inside the full frame and says where it was.
    My prediction, for all three resolutions:
      "horizontal position: as requested;  vertical position: MIRRORED (requested 92, found 554 = 922 - 92 - 276)"
      "crop content mirrored against the full frame: no"
      "RESULT: horizontal ['as'] vertical ['MIRRORED'] ..."
    If it says "as requested" for both, my model is wrong: send me the file and do not go on to Part D2. If a line says "no reliable match", the scene
    has too little texture or something moved: use the tissue paper, more light, and run it again. Send me ~/roitest.log.


PART C - Snaps in a row (command line, about 1 minute)
------------------------------------------------------

C1. python scripts/pi_bringup.py snaptest --count 4 2>&1 | tee ~/snaptest.log
    Expect four lines "snap N: 4912x3684 in X s, stream restarts 1, live frames pulled afterwards: M" with M above 0 and no FAILED.
C2. python scripts/pi_bringup.py snaptest --count 3 --no-restart 2>&1 | tee ~/snaptest_norestart.log
    This switches Scopi's restart off and shows what the SDK does by itself. If the second snap needs a restart you will see "snap 2: ... in 5 s or more, stream
    restarts 1"; if every snap works at once without a restart, tell me. Send me both logs.


PART D - The camera in the app
------------------------------

Start the app with: python -m scopi 2>&1 | tee ~/gui.log
Camera > Camera Panel (Ctrl+Shift+C), Refresh, select "U3CMOS18000KPA [touptek]", Connect, Resolution 1228 x 922, Start live view.
USB cable of the camera pointing to the rear of the microscope for these checks.

D1. Orientation. Both Flip boxes start unticked. Put a printed letter F (or a line of text) on the stage, look at it through the eyepiece, then at the live
    view. They must match. Tell me "matches", or what differs and which box fixes it (none / horizontal / vertical / both).
D2. Crop, with the boxes unticked: drag a rectangle around the letter. The live view must show exactly what you enclosed. Drag a second rectangle on the
    cropped picture: it must narrow to what you enclosed. Click the Crop button (full frame). Now tick Flip horizontally and repeat; untick it, tick
    Flip vertically and repeat; tick both and repeat. Send me "ok" for each of the four, or which one is off and by how much (a screenshot like a.png
    and a-cropped.png of the failing one helps; do not put the movie in git).
D3. Snaps in a row. With the live view running at 1228 x 922: Snap, close the new tab, Snap again, again, again (at least four, without stopping the live view).
    Tell me: does every snap arrive, roughly how many seconds each takes, does the live view carry on by itself afterwards (a short blink is expected),
    and does it still work with a crop active (crop, then Snap: the snap is the full 4912 x 3684 picture).
D4. The Gaussian slider. Quit the camera view if you like (Disconnect). File > Open ~/scopi-test/cells_gray.png, Process > Filters > Gaussian Blur,
    tick Preview. Drag the slider slowly to the right and back: the picture should blur progressively while you drag (about four updates a second). Then type 10
    in the Sigma box and wait a second. Tell me: "follows while dragging", "only after I let go" or "nothing", for the slider and for typing. OK.
D5. Measuring. File > Open the snap of the 0.01 mm slide (or snap it), draw a Line along 1 mm of the scale, Image > Set Scale (Known distance 1, Unit mm), OK.
    Read the status line at the bottom of the window ("Scale: 1 pixel = ... mm (... pixels per mm)") and send it. Draw another line along 0.5 mm and press Ctrl+M:
    the Results row now has Length, Length (px), Angle, Mean, Min, Max, StdDev, X, Y and Unit filled in. Send me that row. Length (px) times the pixel size must equal Length.
D6. Quit (File > Quit). Then: grep -c "VIDEOIO\|textinput" ~/gui.log   (expect 0), and
      python scripts/pi_bringup.py render 2>&1 | grep -c textinput   (expect 0)
